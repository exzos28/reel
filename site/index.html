Pick a film. It plays in seconds.

reel is an open-source app with a torrent engine built in. It streams the film while it fetches it, so you never wait for a download. Made for the TV and the remote, and for phones, tablets and computers too.

reel comes with no content and no sources. You connect your own search source.

What it does

  • Streams, never downloads

    There is no downloads screen. The engine keeps only a buffer of the film on the device, up to the size you set.

  • Continue watching

    Every film and episode resumes where you stopped. Watched episodes get a mark.

  • Seasons and episodes

    Season tabs and an episode list for every series. Near the end of an episode, the next one is ready to start.

  • Only what your device plays

    Releases with audio, video or HDR formats the device cannot play are left out, per device, so a film does not fail to start because of its format.

  • Subtitles and audio tracks

    Pick the audio track and subtitles, from the video or from subtitle files in the release. Your subtitle choice is kept per title.

  • Stream details

    See how fast the film is coming, from how many people, and how many minutes are ready ahead. Off if you prefer a clean screen.

  • Anime

    A separate anime catalog from AniList and Jikan, with trending this season and all-time lists.

  • Browse and search

    Movies, shows and anime, filtered by genre, years and country, sorted by popularity, rating or date. The grid keeps loading as you scroll. Search covers all three.

  • Titles in your language

    Titles, descriptions and episode names come in the language you choose. reel searches releases by the title in that language, then by the original.

Made for the TV first

reel is designed for a room with the lights down and a remote in hand. Phones, tablets and computers get the same app with layouts of their own.

  • Everything works with the four arrows, OK and Back.
  • Focus is a white ring that lifts the item, the same on every screen.
  • Back goes one level up, closes panels first, and asks before leaving the app.
  • Large type and few items per row, readable from the couch.

Samsung TVs (Tizen)

reel runs on Samsung TVs as a sideloaded app, never through the Samsung store. The first test TV is a 2024 model on Tizen 9; other models are not tested yet.

  1. On the TV, open Apps, press 1 2 3 4 5 on the remote and turn on Developer mode, with your computer's IP as Host PC IP. Restart the TV.
  2. On that computer, open Apps2Samsung and pick the TV.
  3. Install the reel .wgt package. Apps2Samsung signs it for your TV.

Open source, no accounts

The whole app is on GitHub: a Rust core with the torrent engine, and one interface for every platform.

  • No reel account and no sign-in.
  • No analytics or tracking code.
  • Settings and watch progress stay on your device.

Who reel talks to

  • Your search source, at the address you entered.
  • TMDB, AniList and Jikan, for titles, posters and descriptions.
  • A public anime ID list on GitHub, to match anime between catalogs.
  • Other people sharing the film. As with any torrent app, they can see your IP address.

Questions

What is reel?

A video player with a torrent engine and a film and series catalog. You pick a title, it finds releases through your search source and streams the one you choose.

Does reel come with films or sources?

No. reel ships no content, no tracker addresses and no indexers. Releases come only from the search source you add in Settings.

What is a search source?

A search service that answers in the Torznab format, which you run and configure yourself, for example Jackett or Prowlarr on your computer or home server. You give reel its address and API key.

What am I allowed to watch?

reel is a tool, like a browser or a torrent client. What you search for and play is your choice and your responsibility. Play only what you have the right to watch where you live.

Does it fill up my device?

No. reel streams and keeps only a buffer of the film, up to the size you set in Settings. It needs that much free space to start.

Where do titles, posters and descriptions come from?

From TMDB for films and series, and from AniList and Jikan for anime. If you build reel yourself, add your own free TMDB key; without one, reel lists only anime.

Why is it not in the App Store or Google Play?

The App Store does not accept torrent clients, and reel is not published to any store. Packages will be on GitHub Releases; Samsung TVs install them through Apps2Samsung.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no account, no subscription and no ads.
